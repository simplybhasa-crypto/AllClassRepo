01 / What it celebrates

A story of courage, a feeling of belonging.

Dashain is celebrated by Nepali communities in Nepal and around the world. For many Hindus, it honours the victory of the goddess Durga over Mahishasura, a story of good overcoming evil. Its meaning and customs can vary between families and communities.

The heart of Dashain

More than a holiday: a reason to come together.

Long journeys home, shared meals, visits with relatives, and time with elders make Dashain a festival of connection as much as celebration.

A symbol of new life

Jamara

Barley seeds are sown at the start of the festival. Their tender yellow-green shoots are cared for and later offered as a blessing.

A message of hope

Victory & renewal

The festival's stories remind people that courage, care, and goodness can overcome hardship.

02 / Little traditions, big memories

What makes Dashain feel like Dashain?

Customs differ from one home to another. Choose a tradition to see a little of what it means to many families.

Tradition 01

Tika & blessings

On Bijaya Dashami, elders place tika, often a mixture of rice, yogurt, and vermilion, and jamara on younger relatives. They offer blessings for health, happiness, and good fortune.

A moment of respect, love, and connection across generations.

03 / A festival in its own rhythm

Fifteen days, each with its own place.

The lunar calendar means the dates shift each year. These are a few of the days many people look forward to during Dashain.

Day 01

Ghatasthapana

The festival begins, and barley seeds are sown to grow jamara.

Day 07

Phulpati

Flowers and sacred offerings mark an important moment in the festivities.

Day 09

Maha Navami

Worship and ceremonies honour Durga on the ninth day.

Day 10

Bijaya Dashami

Families visit elders for tika, jamara, blessings, and time together.

May the season bring everyone a little closer.

Dashain is celebrated in many different ways. The most meaningful traditions are often the ones families share with each other.
