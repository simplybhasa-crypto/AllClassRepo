A tiny cell has a big job

Cell division

Every living thing is made of cells. When a cell grows and divides, it passes a copy of its DNA to new cells—helping organisms grow, repair tissues, and make more cells.

A cell getting ready to divide

First, the cell cycle

Most of a cell’s life is spent growing and preparing. Division is only one part of the full cycle.

G₁The cell grows and carries out its usual work.
SThe cell copies its DNA so each new cell can receive a set.
G₂The cell grows more and checks its copied DNA.
M phaseThe chromosomes separate, then the cell splits.

Remember: Interphase includes G₁, S, and G₂. It is the preparation period—not one of the stages of mitosis.

Mitosis, step by step

Mitosis divides the nucleus. The stages are often remembered as PMAT, followed by cytokinesis.

01

Prophase

DNA coils into visible chromosomes. The nuclear envelope starts to break down, and spindle fibers begin to form.

02

Metaphase

Chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell. Spindle fibers attach to them so they can be moved apart.

03

Anaphase

Sister chromatids separate and are pulled toward opposite ends of the cell. Once separated, each chromatid is considered a chromosome.

04

Telophase

A new nuclear envelope forms around each set of chromosomes. The chromosomes begin to uncoil.

05

Cytokinesis

The cytoplasm divides. In animal cells the membrane pinches inward; in plant cells a new cell plate forms.

06

The result

One parent cell has become two daughter cells, usually with the same number of chromosomes and matching DNA.

One cell becomes two

After mitosis separates the DNA, cytokinesis finishes the split. The new cells can grow and begin the cycle again.

Mitosis: growth and repair

Body cells use mitosis to replace worn-out or damaged cells and to help a multicellular organism grow. It usually makes two genetically similar cells.

Meiosis: making sex cells

Meiosis is a different kind of cell division. It makes sex cells with half the usual chromosome number, so fertilization can restore the full number.

The big idea: DNA is copied before division, then carefully shared. This keeps genetic instructions available in the new cells.
